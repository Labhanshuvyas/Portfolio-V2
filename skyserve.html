SkyServe / Independent product concept

Your Airport, Brought to You.

Making food and essentials easier to reach inside the terminal.

Project typeIndependent exploration
FocusAccessibility & fulfilment design
StatusConcept · Not launched

The concept at a glance

Can delivery be dependable within a passenger’s boarding window?

The visual report presents the broader idea. The thinking below narrows it to an initial scope, a trade-off and a proposed test.

Inside the thinking

01 / PROBLEM & HYPOTHESIS

Start with the decision.

Passengers with limited mobility or little time before boarding may struggle to reach food and essential items across a terminal. Distance, queues and uncertainty can turn a simple purchase into a difficult decision.

Working hypothesis
A limited airport ordering service could improve access if it can reliably fulfil orders within the passenger’s available time, at a cost that works for customers, merchants and the delivery operation.

02 / PROPOSED SCOPE

A smaller first version.

Start here

One terminal, a limited set of categories, mobile web ordering and pickup or delivery to designated locations. Order acceptance depends on available fulfilment time.

Leave for later

Broad service categories, duty-free expansion and multi-airport rollout. A dependable pilot should precede wider operational complexity.

03 / PRODUCT JUDGMENT

Make the trade-off explicit.

More coverage increases choice but also delivery distance and variability. The first service should accept fewer orders reliably rather than promise coverage it cannot fulfil. Delivery charges make convenience and accessibility the core value—not an unsupported claim of lower prices.

04 / PROPOSED VALIDATION

Test usefulness before scale.

I would start with a service simulation and a limited operational pilot, subject to airport and merchant participation. The pilot would track the complete order journey, especially preparation delays, gate changes and failed handoffs.

Primary signal
Orders fulfilled within the promised window, with a clear definition of successful handoff.
Supporting signals
Passenger satisfaction, cancellations and merchant reliability.
Business guardrails
Contribution per order after fulfilment, payment, refund and support costs.

05 / HARDEST UNKNOWN

The assumption to challenge.

The hardest dependency is operational coordination: airport access, merchant preparation, delivery capacity and changing passenger locations. Orders that no longer fit the available time need a clear cancellation and refund process.

06 / NEXT DECISION

What would change the plan?

A successful pilot would establish where the service can make a dependable promise. Category and terminal expansion should follow proven fulfilment performance and sustainable economics.

This is an independent product exploration. Interfaces are illustrative, and the tests described here are proposed. The report’s benefits and roadmap are aspirations, not measured outcomes or commitments from the named companies.
